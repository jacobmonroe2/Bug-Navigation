What the robot does

The robot is given a goal pose (x, y, θ) in its odometry frame and has to drive there on its own, going around anything in the way. We implemented a Bug 2 algorithm: drive straight at the goal along the m-line; when the Lidar shows an obstacle in the way, follow the obstacle's boundary; leave the boundary once the robot is back on the m-line and closer to the goal than where it first hit the obstacle. Because the MBot-Omni can move in any direction, the robot translates without turning and only rotates to the goal heading at the very end.

        v

StateBehaviour
0 · DRIVE_TO_GOALProportional control on the position error, moving straight at the goal.
1 · FOLLOW_WALLProject 1 wall follower: travel tangent to the nearest Lidar ray while holding a 0.35 m stand-off.
2 · TURN_TO_GOALRotate in place to the requested heading.
3 · AT_GOALStop and print the final odometry.

The state is printed every control iteration together with the pose, the distance to the goal and the shortest Lidar ray toward the goal, so transitions can be verified from the terminal.

Detecting obstacles

Checking only the single Lidar ray pointing at the goal misses obstacles that sit just beside that ray. Instead we take the minimum ray inside a 70° slice centred on the goal direction. If that ray is shorter than 0.45 m (and shorter than the distance to the goal, so the object is actually between us and the goal) the goal direction is blocked and the robot switches to wall following. When the robot hits an obstacle it also checks which side of the goal direction the closest ray is on and goes around the other way, so it does not take the long way around.

Discussion

  • Bug 2 vs. Bug 1. Bug 1 circles every obstacle completely before leaving at the closest point, which is safe but slow. Bug 2 leaves as soon as the m-line is re-crossed closer to the goal, which gives much shorter paths on the convex boxes used in the demo course.
  • Odometry drift. Everything is in the odometry frame, so wheel slip during wall following shifts where the robot believes the m-line is. We widened the m-line tolerance and added a fallback leave rule (clear line of sight to the goal and closer than the hit point for several consecutive iterations) so the robot never orbits an obstacle forever.
  • Inside corners. The wall follower uses the single nearest ray as "the wall". At inside corners the nearest ray jumps from one surface to the next; slowing down when a surface appears in the direction of travel made that hand-off smooth.
  • Testing without the robot. We wrote a small 2D simulator with ray-cast Lidar (sim/run_sim.py) and ran the exact navigation code against boxes, walls, a corridor and a U-shaped trap. All scenarios reach the goal within 6 cm and 0.08 rad without collisions, and the printed state sequence matches the state machine above.
